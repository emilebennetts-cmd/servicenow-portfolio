01 / The client's pain & history

Old discovery data should not become a permanent liability.

In a representative, fictional multi-source CMDB, infrastructure changes faster than ownership, attestations and retention controls. Stale servers remain counted as active; discovery may rehydrate retired records; unresolved relationships still feed service maps; and deletion requests arrive before records-management approval.

The legacy response is often a bulk cleanup spreadsheet. It may reduce row counts but can erase a CI that is still referenced by a service or subject to an audit hold.

The governance question

Can we prove the entire disposition path?

A useful policy needs verified class rules, filters, owner assignments, exclusions, dependent-CI checks, approval separation, retention authority and audit evidence. A DEV preview is necessary, but not sufficient, to claim safety.

02 / The ServiceNow solution

Policy-led retirement, with deliberate separation of duties.

ServiceNow CMDB Data Manager provides policy types for Retire, Archive, Delete, Attestation, Certification and related-entry cleanup. In the Australia release, retire/archive/delete policies require active retirement definitions for the target classes. Published policies generate tasks and subflows; depending on policy settings, approvals are required before processing.

Retire

Stop treating a CI as current

Apply class retirement definitions while retaining the record. Investigate source reactivation and downstream processes.

Archive

Preserve a temporary recovery window

Archive already-retired records with a documented retention period. Verify relationships and restoration responsibilities.

Delete

Require explicit disposition authority

Confirm retired state, legal holds and exclusions first. Permanent deletion cannot be presented as a reversible cleanup.
