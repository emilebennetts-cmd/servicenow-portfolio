01 / The client's pain & history

Fragmented investigation

Illustrative ITSM and CMDB scenario: a platform team receives symptoms across several modules, but the evidence is scattered between scripts, exports and manual notes. Repeating checks consumes time and weakens traceability.

Uncontrolled conclusions

Aggregate measurements can be mistaken for a diagnosis. A missing data sample can be misread as a healthy module, and suggested remediation can be mistaken for an approved change.

These are representative operational problems, not documented incidents at a named customer.

03 / Architectural best practices

Separate collection from execution

Read-only collectors produce aggregate evidence. The local-first Workbench processes imported outputs; it does not execute scripts or maintain credentials. This reduces privilege requirements and makes review boundaries explicit.

Keep conclusions provisional

Correlation is a lead for investigation, not proof of causation. Rule-generated solutions remain DRAFT until a qualified reviewer validates scope, risk and change authority.

Protect privacy at intake

Envelope validation refuses consumer data and checks for secrets. These controls are defence-in-depth, not a guarantee that every sensitive field will be detected.

Design for upgradeability

The documented SDK build produces a scoped application UI. Its Jelly page and bundled client assets should be regression-tested after upgrades; avoid claiming OOTB support for custom components.
