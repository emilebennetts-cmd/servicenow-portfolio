Evidence governance

What the Workbench evidence proves — and what it doesn't

A transparent, source-linked guide to evaluating the Universal Delivery and Diagnostic Workbench implementations without confusing repository artefacts, historical instance checks, and production acceptance.

01 / The client's pain & history

Enterprise platform projects accumulate design documents, demonstrations, update sets and test records across multiple releases. Without a version-specific evidence trail, a polished demonstration can be mistaken for a deployed production feature. This is a representative consulting challenge, not a statement about an identified client.

Evidence classification

SOURCE-ONLY

Code, configuration, design or test reports exist in versioned source. This does not establish that the release was installed or exercised in an instance.

DEV-VERIFIED

A dated instance read-back or test record establishes a specific version and environment at a particular point in time. Historical verification is not automatically current.

MOCK-ONLY

Synthetic data or simulated integrations demonstrate intended interactions, not live provider connectivity or customer acceptance.

PRODUCTION-VERIFIED

Requires independently attributable production deployment, acceptance and operational evidence. No production verification is asserted on this page.

03 / Architectural best practices

Use OOTB-first patterns, ITIL 4 continual improvement, scoped application boundaries, least-privilege roles, review gates and explicit release-to-evidence traceability. Treat integrations as optional and entitlement-dependent; do not equate a static UI demonstration with a configured provider connection.

Screenshot publication policy: No actual instance screenshots are included here. Candidate captures require source provenance, owner approval, removal of customer identifiers and secrets, accessibility review, and a verified classification before public publication. A synthetic mock must be visibly labelled as such.
