01 / The client's pain & history

Several discovery feeds. One misleading topology.

In this fictional enterprise estate, historical migration data, cloud inventory and manual CMDB edits create overlapping relationships. A service owner sees a plausible dependency map, but duplicate edges, missing endpoints, stale CIs and inconsistent class directions undermine impact assessments. Fixing the picture without correcting upstream data can cause the same defects to return after reconciliation.

Operational issue

Impact uncertainty

Unreliable dependencies make change risk and incident blast-radius analysis hard to defend.

Data quality

Duplicate and orphan edges

Different ingestion paths create record-level contradictions that are invisible in a high-level topology.

Governance gap

Unclear authority

No accountable source owner approves the relationship type, direction and data-remediation pathway.

03 / Architectural best practices

Native relationship governance before custom scoring.

OOTB-first

Effective relationship rules

Inspect CI Class Manager suggested/dependent relationship definitions and inheritance. A synthetically invalid direction is not proof it is invalid in the target instance.

Performance

Controlled processor scheduling

Check the effective Relationship Compliance Processor job, scope and frequency; relationship health calculations can have operational cost at scale.

Audit-ready

Evidence and replay

Retain exception references and owner decisions privately. Verify ingestion replay, impact consumers, positive and denied personas, and date-bound results.
